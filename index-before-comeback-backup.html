Main Issue · 2/3 — Outsider Leadership · Rebuilding Legacy Credibility

From command-and-control to a shared vision

40 years of one-man control. In 2016 Liberty Media inherited a profitable sport with no infrastructure — and a culture that hadn't moved in decades.

Three outsiders had to earn three kinds of credibility: competence, relational, reliability.

“The organization wasn’t doing the things you needed to do to compete… It was run for short-term cash flow.”

— Chase Carey, CEO, Formula 1
The Old Model

One person, all the decisions

  • Chaotic, opaque decision-making
  • Fractious relationships, little trust
  • No marketing, research, or digital function
  • Dismissed younger fans and new ideas
The New Model

A leadership team with a mandate

  • Clear roles: commercial, brand, motorsport
  • Transparency and feedback with the teams
  • Formal functions built from scratch
  • Evidence-based decisions, freedom to experiment
Main Issue · 2/3 — Incentive Redesign

Rivalry that raises everyone

Three richest teams dominated for four seasons. F1, the FIA, and the teams negotiated a $175m budget cap to level the field.

Unlimited spending rewarded budget size over racecraft — a classic misaligned-incentive problem.

“Competition elevates engagement.” — Sean Bratches

Analysis · 3/3 — Engineering as Collaboration

Marginal gains, made together

A front wing is a thousand small decisions. No individual wins alone — the car is collective intelligence made physical.

High interdependence beats individual expertise — but only when coordination costs are actively managed. Hence: rebuilt as one unit each season, never patched in silos.

Analysis · 3/3 — Strategy & Trust

Split-second calls, built on shared data

At Monaco, the driver trusts a strategy team they cannot see. Decided by analysts, executed by the driver.

Decision-rights design — authority sits with whoever holds the best real-time information, not the formal hierarchy.

Analysis · 3/3 — The Takeaways

Five leadership & teamwork lessons

Set a shared vision before you hire

Hackman’s purpose condition — alignment first, autonomy second.

Blend fresh talent with institutional memory

Belbin’s role balance — new thinking plus retained institutional knowledge.

Replace control with trust and transparency

Trust Equation — raise reliability, lower self-orientation.

Great teamwork is rehearsed, not improvised

The two-second pit stop: swift trust plus rehearsed coordination.

Design competition to lift the whole system

Budget caps realign incentives — teams, partners, and fans all win.

The Inflection Point

“If everything worked exactly as planned, you’re not doing enough.”

Paradox management — control vs. autonomy, speed vs. fairness. Never solved, only continuously managed.
